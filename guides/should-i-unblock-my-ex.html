A setting can become a silent invitation

Should I unblock my ex?

Published and reviewed: September 13, 2026

Direct answer

If you are about to unblock your ex because you hope something will happen afterward, leave the setting unchanged for ten minutes first. Ask, “What am I hoping happens after I unblock them?” If contact is genuinely necessary, define the practical purpose and the boundary you will use before changing the setting.

Why unblocking can feel like doing nothing

Tapping “unblock” looks smaller than sending a message. That is why it can be easy to tell yourself it does not count as contact. But the setting may still carry a private hope: they will notice, view your profile, send something, or prove that they have been trying to reach you.

That hope is not embarrassing. Sometimes blocking was the only way to stop checking, and now the silence feels too final. Sometimes anger has cooled and keeping the block feels harsher than you intended. The useful question is not whether blocking is always right. It is what changing the setting is meant to change today.

Trigger: what happened just before this?

Name the immediate trigger. Did you hear their name from a friend? See an old photo? Feel lonely after a good day? Wonder whether they tried to contact you? The urge may be about the event that happened five minutes ago, not a settled decision about future communication.

If you are repeatedly toggling settings during no contact, read whether blocking supports your no-contact boundary. Blocking and unblocking are tools, not moral verdicts.

Pause: leave the setting alone for ten minutes

Do not open their profile while you wait. Put the exact action into words: “I want to unblock them so that…” Finish the sentence without making it sound reasonable. You may write “so they can reach me,” “so I can check,” or “so I do not feel like the bad person.” Honesty makes the next decision cleaner.

Reality Check: what am I hoping happens next?

Separate the setting from the outcome. If nothing happens after you unblock them, will the decision still feel right? If they message immediately, do you know whether you want to respond? If you start checking again, what boundary will replace the block?

A useful test is: Would I make the same change if I knew they would never notice? A “no” does not forbid unblocking. It shows that the action is partly a message, even if no words are attached.

Next move: choose the boundary before the button

You can keep the block tonight and reconsider on a calmer date. You can unblock while muting, unfollowing, or deciding not to visit their page. You can also unblock for a defined practical reason and use a separate channel for that task. Pick the boundary first so the platform setting does not make the decision for you.

If unblocking is mainly a route back to their profile, use the guide on stopping repeated social-media checking. If your real next move is a message, pause with the decision guide for texting an ex.

When contact may actually be necessary

Property, housing, bills, pets, work, co-parenting, appointments, safety, or a practical handoff can require communication. That does not mean every channel must reopen. Define the task, choose the narrowest suitable channel, and keep the exchange short, factual, specific, and bounded. For legal, safety, or custody questions, use appropriate qualified support.

What not to conclude too quickly

  • Keeping the block tonight does not mean it must remain forever.
  • Unblocking does not require you to follow, check, reply, or reconcile.
  • Their silence after you unblock is not a verdict on your worth.
  • A message after unblocking is information, not automatic evidence of change.

Frequently asked questions

Is it immature to keep an ex blocked?

No single setting proves maturity. Judge it by whether it supports safety, clarity, and the boundary you chose.

Will my ex know I unblocked them?

Platform behavior varies, and they may notice only if they search, message, or revisit your profile. Do not base the decision on trying to control whether they notice.

What if I blocked them during an argument?

You can reconsider when the emotional spike has passed. Decide what communication, if any, you are actually prepared for before changing the setting.
