Handle the task without reopening the relationship

How to text your ex when you actually have to

Published and reviewed: September 8, 2026

Short answer

When you have to text your ex, make the message short, factual, specific, and limited to one practical outcome. State what happened, ask for one clear action, and include a reasonable next step or deadline only when the situation requires it. Remove relationship commentary, hidden tests, blame, and questions that do not help complete the task.

No contact does not make real-life logistics disappear

A package can still arrive. A key may need to be returned. A bill, lease question, work task, child-related responsibility, or item left behind may require coordination. Necessary contact is different from using a practical topic to ask where the relationship stands.

The goal is not to sound cold or win the breakup. It is to solve one real issue with the least emotional spillover. If you are unsure whether the reason is practical or emotional, start with the general decision guide for texting an ex.

This page offers communication structure, not legal, financial, medical, or custody advice. When the issue involves rights, safety, formal deadlines, or unclear responsibility, use appropriate local or qualified guidance.

Use the SHORT framework

  • Short: Keep it to the information required to complete the task. One short paragraph is often enough.
  • Factual: Describe what happened without adding accusations, old arguments, or guesses about intent.
  • Specific: Ask for one action: choose a pickup time, confirm an address, send a document, or answer a defined question.
  • Limited: Keep the thread on the practical topic. Do not add “How have you been?” if you are not ready for a personal conversation.

Before sending, read the message once and delete any sentence that asks for reassurance, creates guilt, or invites a relationship postmortem.

Neutral scripts for necessary contact

A package or mail arrived

“Your package arrived at my address. Let me know by Friday whether you want to pick it up or have it forwarded.”

You need to return keys

“I still have the apartment key. I can leave it with the front desk on Tuesday at 5 p.m. Please confirm whether that works.”

A shared bill needs attention

“The final internet bill is $84 and is due September 15. Your share is $42. Please send it through our usual payment method by the due date.”

You need to exchange belongings

“I have one box of your belongings ready. I can leave it with Jordan on Saturday, or you can suggest another practical handoff option.”

A shared work task needs a decision

“The client needs the final file by Thursday. Please confirm whether you will send your section by Wednesday at noon.”

Replace the details with true information from your situation. Do not invent a deadline to create urgency, and do not use a third party without that person’s agreement.

What to leave out of the message

  • “Since I have to text anyway, I need to know if you miss me.”
  • “If you cared, you would handle this today.”
  • “I guess this proves I always had to do everything.”
  • A photo, memory, or inside joke that has nothing to do with the task.
  • Threats, vague consequences, or a fake emergency.

A practical message can be polite without becoming intimate. “Thanks” and “please confirm” are enough. You do not need to perform indifference, and you do not need to explain every feeling.

Choose the narrowest channel and time

Use the channel already associated with the responsibility when possible: email for documents, a co-parenting system for agreed child-related coordination, or a text thread for a simple pickup. Avoid moving the conversation to social media or opening several channels at once.

Send during ordinary daytime hours unless the issue is genuinely urgent. Then check for a response at a planned time rather than watching the typing indicator or repeatedly reopening the thread. When belongings are the issue

List the items, choose a handoff method, and keep the message about access and timing. Do not use a sweatshirt, box, or piece of mail as a reason to create an in-person emotional conversation. The detailed guide to packages, keys, and belongings after a breakup covers different item types and neutral handoffs.

Pause before sending even when contact is necessary

Necessary does not always mean immediate. Put the draft down for ten minutes unless a genuine deadline or safety need prevents that. During the pause, confirm the facts, remove emotional additions, and ask whether the recipient can understand exactly what action you need.

If you already sent too much, do not send three more messages explaining the first one. Return to the practical topic. The guide for what to do after breaking no contact can help stop the follow-up spiral.

Frequently asked questions

Should I say hello before the practical message?

A simple “Hi” is fine. You do not need a long warm-up. Move directly to the factual reason for contact.

What if my ex ignores a necessary message?

Use one concise follow-up only when the task and timing justify it. If the matter involves legal rights, money, custody, safety, or a formal deadline, seek appropriate professional guidance instead of escalating through emotional texts.

Can I ask a friend to handle the exchange?

Yes, if the friend freely agrees and the arrangement is safe and practical. Tell your ex exactly how the handoff will work and keep the friend out of the relationship conflict.
