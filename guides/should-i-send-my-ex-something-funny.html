Sometimes you miss the person who understood the joke

Should I send my ex something funny?

Published and reviewed: September 13, 2026

Direct answer

The meme, clip, or inside joke may be harmless, but the conversation it reopens may not feel harmless afterward. Ask, “Am I sharing the joke, or am I hoping the conversation starts again?” Save it, wait ten minutes, and decide only after you can accept a laugh, a cold reply, or no reply.

Why this urge feels so ordinary

A funny post can bypass every serious reason you stopped talking. It does not look like a confession or a request to get back together. It looks like two seconds of shared humor. But part of what you may miss is having one person who instantly understood the reference, the strange clip, or the exact kind of joke you would send without explanation.

Missing that shorthand is real. It does not automatically mean reopening the conversation will give it back. The old rhythm may not exist now, and even a warm response can leave you waiting for the next message.

Trigger: notice the imagined reaction

Before sharing, notice the scene already playing in your head. Are they laughing? Replying with another meme? Saying they miss this? Is the joke functioning as a low-risk test of whether they still welcome you?

If you have been collecting posts specifically for them, the behavior may be closer to drafting messages than casual sharing. The guide on re-reading old messages can help when you are trying to recreate the feeling of an old conversation.

Pause: save it somewhere else

Bookmark it, send it to yourself, or copy the link into a private note. Do not leave it hovering over their name in the share sheet. Set a ten-minute timer and move away from the app. Saving the content preserves the joke without turning the first impulse into contact.

Reality Check: am I sharing the joke or reopening the door?

Ask what result would make sending feel “worth it.” If the honest answer is any response at all, the message is doing more than sharing humor. Then ask whether you can tolerate each realistic outcome: no reply, a reaction emoji, a short laugh, an extended conversation, or a clear request for space.

Another useful question is: Would I send this if replies were disabled? If yes, sharing may genuinely be the point. If no, the hoped-for conversation deserves to be named before you act.

Next move: decide after the joke stops feeling urgent

After ten minutes, you can keep it unsent, share it with someone else, or send it if contact is welcome and you can accept the likely outcomes. There is no rule that every funny thing must disappear with the relationship. There is also no need to use humor as a disguised request for reassurance.

If you want to add “this made me think of you,” pause with the broader question whether texting your ex helps the situation you are in. If the urge arrives late at night, use the guide for missing your ex at night before choosing an audience.

When sending may be reasonable

Sharing may be uncomplicated when both people have clearly agreed to friendly contact, the breakup has settled, and you are not using the joke to test the relationship. Even then, you can choose not to send. Permission to contact is not an obligation to keep an old conversational habit alive.

What not to conclude from the response

  • A laugh does not prove the relationship is reopening.
  • No reply does not make the joke or you embarrassing.
  • A quick exchange does not require a second message.
  • Feeling understood for a moment does not settle what happens next.

Frequently asked questions

Is sending a meme breaking no contact?

If your boundary is no direct contact, a meme is still contact. The format does not cancel the agreement you made with yourself or the other person.

What if the meme is about something only they understand?

That explains why the urge is strong. Save it privately and decide whether being understood is worth reopening the current boundary.

Should I delete everything I saved for them?

Not necessarily. Move it out of the immediate share flow and review it later, when each item no longer feels like a reason to reach out.
