Before the next search

How do I stop checking my ex’s social media?

To stop checking your ex’s social media, make the next check harder and give the urge somewhere else to go. Mute, unfollow, remove search history, move the app, and set a short delay before opening it. Then replace the check with one action that ends—write what you hoped to find, take a short walk, or text a safe friend.

Published and reviewed: September 4, 2026

Why the check feels useful for a few seconds

You are usually not looking for a photo. You are looking for certainty: whether they miss you, whether they moved on, whether the breakup meant what you fear it meant. A profile offers fragments, so your mind keeps returning for the missing answer. The check briefly ends not-knowing, then creates new details to interpret.

A peer-reviewed study of Facebook surveillance after breakups found an association between monitoring an ex and greater breakup distress and longing. That does not mean one look causes a specific outcome. It does explain why “I will just check once” may not deliver the relief it promises.

Step 1: name the cue before changing the app

For three days, note what happened immediately before you searched. Common cues include waking up, getting into bed, seeing a mutual friend, feeling lonely after work, drinking, or noticing that your own post has views. Keep the note simple: time, cue, action, and what you hoped to learn.

This is not a scorecard for shaming yourself. It shows where the loop begins. If the cue is always the phone beside your pillow, the first change is physical. If the cue is a mutual friend’s post, the first change may be muting that account for a while.

Step 2: add friction to the exact route you use

  • Mute or unfollow the account and mutual-friend updates that reliably lead you there.
  • Clear recent searches, saved links, screenshots, and browser bookmarks.
  • Move the app off your home screen or sign out on the device you use at night.
  • Use an app limit or website blocker during your highest-risk hours.
  • If smaller limits repeatedly fail, consider whether blocking your ex during no contact is the clearer boundary.

Friction is not weakness. It keeps a two-second impulse from becoming a twenty-minute investigation. Harvard Health’s guidance on changing habits similarly emphasizes identifying triggers and changing the routine around them.

Step 3: replace checking with an action that has an ending

“Do not check” leaves an empty space. Fill it with a small action you can complete: write one sentence about what you hoped to see, drink a glass of water, walk to another room, or send a neutral message to a friend. Choose something with a clear finish rather than another endless feed.

If the urge is tied to a message you want to send, use the ten-minute decision guide before texting an ex. If it rises mostly after dark, the steps in what to do when you miss your ex at night can help you change the setting around the urge.

What to do after you check again

Do not turn one check into permission to spend the rest of the night searching. Close the app, record the cue, and restore the boundary. A lapse gives you information: perhaps the app limit was too easy to dismiss, or you kept a second route open. Adjust that route instead of declaring the whole plan failed.

A useful first goal is not “I will never look again.” Try one protected evening, then one protected morning. Build evidence that an urge can rise and pass without being answered.

Frequently asked questions

Should I unfollow or block my ex?

Start with the boundary that actually stops the route you use. Muting or unfollowing may be enough when exposure is accidental. Blocking may be more useful when you keep searching manually or contact is unwanted.

Why do I check even when I know it will hurt?

The check offers a quick possibility of certainty or relief. Because the information is incomplete, it often creates more questions. That is a learned loop, not proof that you secretly need to reconnect.

Should I ask friends not to update me?

Yes, if updates restart the same monitoring cycle. Give friends a specific request: “Please do not send me screenshots or news about them for the next month.”

What if I checked again after several good days?

Close the app and restart from the next minute. Note the cue and strengthen that one weak point. One check does not erase the days you protected.
