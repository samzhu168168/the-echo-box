The date can be meaningful without becoming a message

Should I text my ex on our anniversary?

Published and reviewed: September 8, 2026

Short answer

An anniversary reminder by itself is not necessarily a reason to reopen contact. You can remember what the relationship meant without asking your ex to respond to that memory. Before texting, decide what practical outcome you need, whether you can accept no reply, and whether the message respects the boundary already in place.

Why anniversaries create such a strong urge to contact an ex

An anniversary gives the day a script. Your mind knows what you used to do, what you might have planned, and what the date was supposed to mean. A calendar reminder, photo memory, restaurant, song, or quiet evening can make the old routine feel briefly current again.

That feeling does not prove the relationship should restart. It means the date still carries an association. The urge often asks for immediate relief: send something, see whether they remember, or make the day feel less one-sided. Contact may offer a few minutes of certainty, but it can also create a new wait for a reply.

This is similar to the question behind texting an ex on their birthday: a meaningful date can explain the urge without deciding what you should do.

Ask these three questions before you text

  1. What outcome am I actually asking for? Name the result in plain language. Acknowledgment? A conversation? Reassurance? A practical answer? If the real hope is that one message will reveal how they feel, the anniversary is carrying more weight than a simple greeting.
  2. Can I handle every likely response? Include a warm reply, a short reply, no reply, or a request for more distance. If one of those outcomes would pull you into repeated checking or follow-up messages, wait.
  3. Would I send this on an ordinary Tuesday? If the reason disappears when you remove the date, you are probably responding to the anniversary cue rather than a current need.

If you still cannot tell, use the broader decision guide for whether to text your ex before opening their chat.

When no contact is probably still appropriate

Keeping no contact is usually the cleaner choice when the message is mainly meant to test whether they remember, invite nostalgia, get reassurance, or create an opening without saying that directly. It may also be better to stay quiet when either person has asked for space or when previous contact repeatedly turned into arguments, bargaining, or days of anxious waiting.

No contact does not require pretending the date means nothing. You can write down what you miss, what you learned, and what you wish had happened without turning those thoughts into a request for your ex. A private ritual can mark the day without reopening communication.

When contact may actually be necessary

The date itself is not a practical emergency. Contact may still be necessary if the same day includes a real responsibility involving children, shared property, travel, work, bills, safety, or a deadline. Keep that message limited to the responsibility. Do not combine a logistical request with “I know what today is” unless both people have already agreed that personal conversation is welcome.

If you need to communicate about something concrete, use a short, factual, specific, and limited message. For legal, financial, custody, or safety questions, use appropriate local or professional guidance rather than relying on a breakup guide.

Use a ten-minute pause before deciding

Write the exact anniversary message somewhere it cannot send. Then set a ten-minute timer and answer three lines: “What do I want back?” “What happens if they do not reply?” and “What choice will feel clean tomorrow morning?” Do something physical while the timer runs—stand up, drink water, or leave the phone in another room.

The goal is not to erase the feeling. It is to keep the strongest minute from making the whole decision.

What if they already texted you?

Their message still does not require an immediate answer. Read what was actually written rather than what the anniversary makes you hope it means. Is it a simple acknowledgment, a practical question, an apology, or an invitation to talk? You can pause, choose not to reply, or send a brief response that matches your boundary.

If the message arrived during a no-contact period, use the guide for what to do when your ex texts during no contact. If you already replied and regret it, one message does not erase your progress; stop the follow-up spiral and choose the next boundary.

Frequently asked questions

What if we both always celebrated this date?

A shared tradition explains why the day feels charged, but it does not establish a current agreement to communicate. Use your present boundary, not the old routine, to decide.

Is a simple “thinking of you” message harmless?

It may be brief, but it is not emotionally neutral if you are hoping for reassurance or renewed contact. Judge the message by the outcome you want and the responses you can tolerate.

What can I do instead of texting?

Write the unsent message, remove the calendar reminder for next year, spend the hour with someone safe, or make one plan that belongs to your current life.
